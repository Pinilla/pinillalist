# pinillalist v1 spec

Status: draft, 2026-11-08. Written from the decisions on the [wayfinder map](https://github.com/Pinilla/pinillalist/issues/1). Each section links the ticket that holds its detail. Domain terms (Grocery, Item, Ingredient, Stock check and the rest) are defined in [`GLOSSARY.md`](../../GLOSSARY.md), and this spec uses them with those meanings.

Three decisions are still open. Each one has an interim default here, marked **Open**, and is collected in [Open decisions](#open-decisions) at the end.

## What v1 is

Pinillalist is a self-hosted grocery list and recipe app for one household: the owner and their wife. It replaces AnyList. It runs at https://list.pinillaweb.org on server1, and people use it mostly on their phones.

The app is built around the Saturday **weekly order**. The household picks recipes for the week, checks what's already at home, adds essentials and anything else, and then fills the Walmart and Amazon carts with one tap per store. Each grocery in the catalog links to one exact product, such as one particular deli American cheese, so nobody has to search the store for it again.

### In scope

- Recipes, imported once from AnyList, and editable afterward.
- The household catalog of groceries, each optionally linked to one Walmart or Amazon product.
- The Essentials list and other grocery lists.
- The weekly order wizard, and filling store carts through add-to-cart links.
- One household password.

### Out of scope for v1

- Stores other than Walmart and Amazon.
- Separate logins per person, and public sign-up.
- A native app-store app.
- Ongoing sync with AnyList. The import happens once ([Export the household's AnyList data](https://github.com/Pinilla/pinillalist/issues/9)).
- History of past weekly orders ([How the two phones share the weekly order](https://github.com/Pinilla/pinillalist/issues/12)).
- Placing orders, checking out, or controlling a store account. A person always checks out in the store's own app.
- After v1, as an idea from the owner: choosing recipes by swiping left and right through them, one at a time.

## Data

This is the shape of the data, not the final schema.

- **Grocery**: name, unit (the unit the household counts and buys in, such as "onion", "lb" or "pack"), and an optional linked **Product**. Every grocery belongs to the catalog.
- **Product**: store (Walmart or Amazon), the store's ID (Walmart item ID or Amazon ASIN), a name, and optionally a size, a picture URL and how it is sold (for example "by the pound" for sliced-to-order deli items). One grocery links to at most one product, and the product's store decides which cart it goes to ([How amounts merge and turn into cart quantities](https://github.com/Pinilla/pinillalist/issues/6), [Test the store cart links on our phones](https://github.com/Pinilla/pinillalist/issues/11)).
- **Recipe**: name, collections (such as Lunches or Meal Prep), servings, prep time, cook time, steps, note, source name and URL, rating, and a photo.
- **Ingredient**: belongs to a recipe and points at a grocery. It has a cooking amount as free text ("1 cup") and an optional preparation ("diced"). Section headings inside a recipe's ingredient list ("For the sauce") are kept as headings.
- **Grocery list**: a name and its items. The **Essentials list** is a standing list whose items never get "done".

Recipes, the catalog, products and lists are shared household data. Both phones see the same data, and if two people edit the same thing at once, the last save wins ([How the two phones share the weekly order](https://github.com/Pinilla/pinillalist/issues/12)).

## The weekly order wizard

The wizard is the main screen. It has five steps, in this order ([The Saturday flow on a phone](https://github.com/Pinilla/pinillalist/issues/5)). A progress bar shows the step, and Back and Next buttons move between steps. Every step can be skipped, so someone who only wants to add a few things can jump straight to step 4. The chosen layout is variant A of the [prototype](https://list.pinillaweb.org/prototypes/saturday-order.html).

### 1. Pick recipes

- Shows every recipe with its photo, filterable by collection.
- Tapping a recipe selects or unselects it, and the step shows how many are chosen.

### 2. Stock check

The app merges the chosen recipes' ingredients into one item per grocery, so "diced onion" and "onion" are both Onion. It doesn't add the amounts up. On 2026-11-11 the owner dropped buying amounts ([Reword the amounts in the stock check](https://github.com/Pinilla/pinillalist/issues/17)), which replaces the earlier decision in [How amounts merge and turn into cart quantities](https://github.com/Pinilla/pinillalist/issues/6).

The stock check shows one item at a time on a large card:

- The grocery's name and its linked product.
- A bulleted list of every ingredient line that asks for it, such as "2 diced, for Chili" and "1 cup, for Tacos".
- A quantity of the product to buy, starting at 1. The app never converts amounts into packages. The person reads the lines and decides.
- **Next** saves the quantity and moves on. **Have it**, or taking the quantity down to 0, buys none and moves on.
- A Previous item button returns to the previous card.

After the last card, a summary lists every item with its quantity, and any item can be changed there.

### 3. Essentials

- Shows the Essentials list.
- The person taps the few items that are low this week, and enters a product quantity for each, starting at 1.

### 4. Anything else

- A search box over the catalog, for things like items for the 2-year-old.
- Typing a name that isn't in the catalog offers to add it as a new grocery.
- Each added item gets a product quantity, starting at 1.

### 5. Fill carts

- Shows one section per store that has items, listing them.
- Each section has a "Fill my Walmart cart" or "Fill my Amazon cart" button that opens the store's add-to-cart link (see [Store carts](#store-carts)).
- Items whose grocery has no linked product can't go into a cart link. They are listed separately so the person can add them in the store app by hand.

### Two phones

Each phone runs its own wizard, and the phones never sync a weekly order. Both phones are signed in to the same Walmart account and the same Amazon account, so the store cart combines everything both people send. If both add the same thing, the store cart gets both quantities, and the household fixes that in the store app before checkout ([How the two phones share the weekly order](https://github.com/Pinilla/pinillalist/issues/12)).

Wizard progress is saved on the phone itself, so closing the app resumes where the person left off. The server doesn't store wizard progress, and with one shared login it couldn't tell the two people apart anyway.

## Store carts

The app fills carts with each store's own add-to-cart link. It never signs in to a store, stores a store password, or drives a browser on the server, because both stores' terms forbid automated agents ([How to fill Walmart and Amazon carts from a list of products](https://github.com/Pinilla/pinillalist/issues/2)).

- **Walmart:** `https://www.walmart.com/sc/cart/addToCart?items=<itemId>_<qty>,<itemId>_<qty>`. On the household's phones this opens the Walmart app straight to the cart, with no confirmation step. For sliced-to-order deli products the quantity is in pounds, so `_1` adds 1 lb. One invalid item ID makes Walmart show an error and go to its home page instead of the cart, so only items with a numeric item ID go in the link.
- **Amazon:** `https://www.amazon.com/gp/aws/cart/add.html?ASIN.1=<asin>&Quantity.1=<qty>&ASIN.2=...`, with no Associate tag. This opens the Amazon app on a confirmation page, and one tap adds everything. Amazon no longer documents this link, so it may stop working without notice. The household must not add its own Associate tag, because Amazon forbids buying through your own affiliate links.
- **Not yet tested:** a Walmart link with a full order of about 40 items. Check it once real products are linked.

## Linking groceries to products

**Open:** [How you link a grocery to a product](https://github.com/Pinilla/pinillalist/issues/7).

What's decided:

- Pasting a product link always works. For Walmart, the item ID is the number after `/ip/`, which also covers links shared from the Walmart app (`walmart.com/ip/<id>?sid=...`). For Amazon, the ASIN is the 10-character code after `/dp/` or `/gp/product/` ([How to look up a product's details from a Walmart or Amazon link](https://github.com/Pinilla/pinillalist/issues/4)).
- The owner wants a **linking wizard** that walks the catalog one grocery at a time and offers matching Walmart products to pick from, because pasting a link for hundreds of groceries would take too long. That needs the Walmart Affiliate API's search endpoint, which is waiting on [Try Walmart's product lookup on our groceries](https://github.com/Pinilla/pinillalist/issues/10).
- Amazon has no API a household can use, so Amazon products are always linked by pasting a link and typing a name and size.

Interim default for v1: link by pasting a link and typing a name. Add the Walmart search wizard once the API works.

## Unavailable products

**Open:** [What happens when a linked product is unavailable](https://github.com/Pinilla/pinillalist/issues/13).

Interim default: no stock checks. If Walmart rejects a cart link, the person removes the bad item in the app and taps the button again.

## AnyList import

The import is a one-time job, run as a script on server1 and not offered as a feature in the app ([Export the household's AnyList data](https://github.com/Pinilla/pinillalist/issues/9)). Its input is the export already saved in `~/anylist-export/`:

- `user-data.json`: the decoded account data, with 76 recipes in 7 collections and 4 lists.
- `photos/`: 66 recipe photos.

The import does the following:

1. Creates each recipe with its collections, servings, times, steps, note, source and rating, and copies its photo. AnyList stores `prepTime` and `cookTime` in seconds.
2. For each ingredient, splits AnyList's text into a grocery name, a cooking amount and a preparation. AnyList's `quantity` holds the amount and unit together as text: `3/4 tsp`, `½ teaspoon`, `.125 tsp`, `1 cup (113 g)`, `2 cloves`, `1 large`, `Pinch`. 219 of 861 ingredients have no quantity. Preparations appear either in `note` (`crushed`) or inside the name (`scallions (sliced)`).
3. Matches ingredients to groceries by name, creating the catalog as it goes, and puts near-duplicates ("onion", "yellow onion") in front of the owner to merge or keep apart.
4. Gives each grocery a unit where its cooking amounts already count it ("1 lb", "2 cans").
5. Creates the Essentials list from AnyList's "Weekly Essentials" (29 items). It skips "Quick Things", which holds personal to-dos rather than groceries. On 2026-11-08 the owner chose to leave out "Store" (3 items) and the empty "My Grocery List" too.

## Login

- The app has its own login page with one household password, set when the app is installed on server1 ([Choose the tech stack](https://github.com/Pinilla/pinillalist/issues/8)).
- After logging in, a phone stays signed in for months through a long-lived cookie.
- Every page and API route except the login page requires that cookie.

## Tech stack

From [Choose the tech stack](https://github.com/Pinilla/pinillalist/issues/8):

- **Language:** TypeScript for both server and UI.
- **Runtime:** Node.js 24 LTS from the official release, unpacked into `/opt/node` and upgraded by hand. Not from apt, because server1's apt sources mix in Debian unstable. npm is the package manager.
- **Server:** Hono, serving the API and the built UI from one process on one port.
- **UI:** a React single-page app built with Vite, installable to the phone's home screen through a web app manifest.
- **Database:** SQLite through Drizzle. The schema lives in TypeScript, and schema changes are migration files.
- **Tests:** Vitest for logic and API tests, each test with its own throwaway SQLite file. Playwright runs a few complete wizard walks in headless Chromium and checks the cart links the app builds, without ever calling a store. GitHub Actions runs both on every pull request.

Logic that needs tests first, because mistakes there would reach the store cart:

- Merging ingredients into one item per grocery.
- Parsing AnyList quantities and preparations.
- Parsing item IDs and ASINs from pasted links.
- Building the Walmart and Amazon cart links, including leaving out unlinked items.

## Running on server1

- A systemd service, `pinillalist.service`, runs as its own `pinillalist` system user and listens only on `127.0.0.1`.
- nginx passes `list.pinillaweb.org` to it, through the existing `#PINILLALIST` block in `/etc/nginx/conf.d/http.conf`. `/prototypes/` keeps serving `/var/www/pinillalist/prototypes/`.
- The database and photos live in `/var/lib/pinillalist/`, which gets added to the nightly restic backup.
- A deploy script on server1 pulls `main`, runs the tests, builds, copies the database, applies migrations and restarts the service. The owner reviews and merges every pull request, and Claude runs the deploy when the owner asks. Nothing deploys automatically from GitHub.
- Follow `~/homelab/CLAUDE.md` for any change to server1, and record it in `~/homelab/changelog.md` and `~/homelab/services/pinillalist.md`.

### Assumptions to confirm

These details came up while writing the spec and were never discussed:

- The Essentials step and the Anything else step ask for a product quantity, starting at 1, the same way the stock check does.
- Grocery lists other than Essentials (such as AnyList's "Store" list) exist in v1, but the wizard doesn't read them. If their items should join the weekly order, that becomes a wizard step or part of Anything else.
